Many Sammamish, WA homeowners replacing an aging furnace and AC are choosing a single heat pump instead, and the reason is efficiency. A heat pump does not burn fuel or heat a resistance coil; it moves heat. In cooling mode it carries heat out of the house, and in heating mode a reversing valve sends the refrigerant the other way so the compressor gathers heat from outdoor air and releases it indoors. That lets it deliver two to three times more heat than the electricity it consumes. Modern cold-climate equipment with inverter-driven compressors keeps working well below freezing, a genuine engineering leap over earlier generations.

The design phase decides whether a heat pump delivers or disappoints. Unlike a furnace or an AC alone, it must handle both the cooling load and the heating load, so we calculate each one and locate the balance point, the outdoor temperature below which the unit needs help. From there we match compressor staging to your climate and ducts and design the backup heat: electric strips that stage in only when needed, or a dual-fuel pairing where a gas furnace handles the coldest stretches. Electrical capacity and duct airflow get verified too, because the lower supply temperature of a heat pump only feels right with enough air moving.

The installation work is where careful habits pay off. We pressure-test and evacuate the line set to a verified vacuum, weigh in the charge and confirm it with superheat and subcooling, and raise the outdoor unit on a stand when your winters call for defrost drainage. The condensate gets proper handling, and the thermostat is set so staging, backup heat lockouts, and defrost behave exactly as the engineering intends. Why Professional Heat Pump Installation Matters

Since a heat pump runs in both seasons, installation mistakes cost you twice. An incorrect charge cuts cooling efficiency in summer and heating capacity in winter. Skip the balance point calculation and electric backup strips may end up heating the house at several times the operating cost, leaving the homeowner puzzled about why the bills never dropped. Leave the thermostat on factory defaults and it can call for backup heat the compressor could have handled, or interfere with defrost. Set the outdoor unit flat on the ground in a real winter and it sits in refrozen defrost water until the coil turns to ice.

We design and install to prevent every one of those problems. Loads are calculated for heating and cooling, equipment is selected for its actual capacity at your winter design temperature, backup heat is staged and locked out properly, the outdoor unit is raised for defrost drainage, and the charge is weighed and verified instead of guessed. Commissioning covers both modes, including a check of the reversing valve and a full defrost cycle. Refrigerant handling is federally regulated, and inverter equipment brings electronics that reward attention to detail. How to Prevent Heat Pump Installation Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Check your first winter bill, since an unexpected jump often points to overworked backup heat
  • Plan two tune-ups a year so the system is verified in both heating and cooling
  • Avoid deep thermostat setbacks, which can call on costly backup heat during recovery
  • A little winter frost is normal; a unit encased in ice is not, so give us a call
  • Change filters on schedule; low airflow hurts a heat pump in both seasons
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow drifts, leaves, and shrubs year-round
  • Gently hose off the outdoor coil every spring before cooling season
