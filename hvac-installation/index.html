A complete HVAC installation is really three jobs wearing one name: choosing equipment that matches your home, making the duct system able to deliver what that equipment produces, and commissioning the result so it performs on the meter and not just on paper. Where most installations go wrong is skipping straight to the equipment swap, bolting a new condenser and furnace or air handler onto whatever came before. Sammamish HVAC starts where the physics starts: a load calculation built from your Sammamish, WA home's square footage, insulation, windows, orientation, and air leakage, because an oversized system short-cycles and dehumidifies poorly while an undersized one never catches up.

Once the load is known, we pick equipment to fit it, and we pick it as a matched set. Manufacturers rate the outdoor unit, indoor coil, and blower together, so a mismatched pairing quietly gives up the SEER2 and AFUE figures printed on the sticker. You will get a plain-language explanation of what single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed equipment actually do, where an ECM blower earns its cost, and whether a furnace, air handler, or heat pump makes the most sense for your house, with no pressure attached. The ducts get the same scrutiny. Our crew measure static pressure, track down leaky or undersized runs, and fix the chokepoints while everything is open, since new equipment on bad ducts never reaches its rating.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. We pressure-test the line set and pull it down to a vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, weigh in the refrigerant charge and check it against superheat and subcooling, leak-test every gas connection, and run the venting exactly to the manufacturer's spec. The condensate line gets a proper trap and a float switch, and the thermostat is set up for the staging your equipment actually has. Before the old equipment leaves your driveway, we commission the system, record temperature split, temperature rise, amp draws, and static pressure, and walk you through all of it. Ready to start? Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Installation?

An HVAC system is the largest machine in most homes, and installation is where its entire service life gets decided. The failure modes are invisible at handoff: a vacuum never verified means moisture breeding acid in the refrigerant loop, a charge set by feel means efficiency lost for good, mismatched coil-and-condenser pairings mean the rated numbers were never achievable, and duct restrictions mean the blower spends fifteen years straining against static pressure it was never designed for. Refrigerant work is federally regulated and gas and venting mistakes are safety problems, not just performance problems.

Our experienced technicians treat installation as a chain of proofs, not a list of tasks. The load calculation proves the size. Matched equipment makes the rating reachable. A pressure test and a micron-gauge vacuum prove the circuit is tight and dry, while a weighed charge checked against superheat and subcooling proves the refrigerant side. On the heating side, gas lines are leak-tested, draft is verified, and blower speed is set to hold the rated temperature rise. Finally, commissioning puts the numbers on paper, creating the baseline every future visit is measured against. That documentation is standard on every system we install in Sammamish.

HVAC Installation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Flush the drain line before summer to keep algae from clogging it
  • Schedule two tune-ups a year: cooling in spring, heating in fall
  • Avoid closing registers, which upsets the airflow balance set at startup
  • Address small noises and performance drifts early, while they are still small
  • Give the outdoor unit clear space on every side and a gentle rinse each season
  • Save your commissioning report and share it with whoever services the system
  • Change filters on schedule from day one; airflow protects both the coil and the exchanger
